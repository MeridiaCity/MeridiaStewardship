# Constitution of Wilsboro

This is the constitution of the first borough in Meridia. This document goes along with a discussion document that provides context and justifications for many of the provisions herein. 

## 10. City council

* **10.A** Three council members are selected using [reweighted range voting](https://www.rangevoting.org/RRV.html). The vote weight of each council member is determined by the ratio of the initial score that council member received (before any reweighting) divided by the sum of those initial scores for all winning candidates.
* **10.B** Each council member must live in the borough they were elected in for greater than 50% of the year. 
* **10.C** Council members serve for 4 years, and may not serve more than 2 consecutive terms nor more than 4 terms in total.

## Powers and limitations

### 20. Legislative branch

**20.A General structures:**

There are two legislative bodies: the council and local committees.

* **20.A.10** The borough council is made up of the same 3 council members the borough elects for city council. 
* **20.A.20** The local committees are the same as are used for city legislative actions.
* **20.A.30** The rules for the borough legislative bodies are the same as for the city's, excepting the differing legislative powers.

**20.B Structure of local committees:**

* **20.B.10** Each block section of city designated travel way will have a local committee that officially meets on self-determined days. Residents and property holders bordering the relevant block's public way may join meetings.
* **20.B.20** Each resident and land holder may only vote for city and borough issues as part of a local committee for 1 block. 
* **20.B.30** Any given local committee may make official decisions on no more than 7 days per year.
* **20.B.40** Votes are done in liquid democracy fashion where each person can register a delegate with the borough government for each of a number of categories. In the case they do not vote, or vote to explicitly delegate their vote for a particular issue or ballot, their relevant vote plus any votes delegated to them will be given to their specified delegate. Issues will all be labeled with a category which will determine how to delegate. The following categories can be used:
  * Representatives - This is a vote for government officials. 
  * Property rights
  * Courts and police
  * Land use
  * Levies and externalities
  * Other
* **20.B.50** Any vote to take an official action during official meeting days is not final until either 1 week has passed or a ballot is made available to everyone who did not attend or could not vote in the meeting with the option to place their votes on the proposals passed by attending citizens.

**20.C Limitations to Powers:**

All powers and limitations designated for boroughs in the City Constitution are available with the following addendums:

* **20.C.20** Zones may only have differentiated ordinances for the following:
  * Energy and matter emitted into or blocked from entering a public space or into a 3rd party's private space.
  * Building height limits.
* **20.C.30** Change in zoning ordinances or zones must compensate land holders for any harm caused by the change in zoning, or temporarily exempt them from the change until such compensation can be produced. 
* **20.C.40** **Legislative change limits**. 
  * **20.C.40.A** Up to 2000 words of ordinance can be proposed per year by each legislative body.
  * **20.C.40.B**  Up to 4000 words of ordinance can be proposed for repeal per year. This includes bills to amend a ordinance which only includes striking line items.
  * **20.C.40.C** Up to 2000 words of Freestead limitation can be proposed per year by each legislative body.
  * **20.C.40.D** Up to 200 words of constitutional change can be proposed per year by each legislative body.
* **20.C.50** A preponderance of evidence must show that an ordinance is expected to produce more total benefits than total costs (including non-monetary ones) before it can be enacted into the ordinances.
* **20.C.60** No revenue will be used by the borough except for levies and borrowed funds. Any seized property, fines, or anything else must be either destroyed or sold at public auction, with the proceeds distributed either to a party owed compensation relevant to the fee or evenly to the people of the borough. 
* **20.C.70** No money may be borrowed if levy revenue can cover the cost of expenditures.
* **20.C.80** **Economic assistance**. Any welfare program created may only be in the form of a universal basic income. To take advantage of any welfare program created, a person must have been a resident of the borough for at minimum 1 year and must have been a resident of the borough within the last year.
* **20.C.90** Any left over ground revenue will be deposited in a reserve fund. If the reserve fund exceeds 2 times the levy revenue collected the prior year, then remaining left over land value levy revenue will be distributed equally to citizens as a citizens dividend. 
* The following regulatory methods of correcting positive and negative externalities are the only ones allowed:
  * **20.C.100** Subsidies based on metrics that can be credibly used to estimate net positive externality produced. 
  * **20.C.140** Levies based on metrics that can be credibly used to estimate net negative externalities produced.
  * **20.C.150** Land value levies based on metrics that can be credibly used to estimate net positive externalities conferred onto the land.
  * **20.C.160** Contests where citizens expresses preferences on what parties to reward for positive externalities conferred and/or how much to reward each. 
  * **20.C.170** Reimbursing taxes from levels of government above the city level in the form of direct payments or in the form of levy reductions. 
* The following regulatory methods of ensuring healthy market competition are the only ones allowed:
  * **20.C.180** Subsidizing a second competitor in the markets of arbitration services and security services only if that second competitor is not the market leader by number of customers nor revenue nor profit and there is no third competitor that meets enacted requirements.  
  * **20.C.190** Block management monopolex (BMM):
    * **20.C.190.A** Management of each segment of public ways is given collectively to the holders of land bordering to the public way (the *BMM stakeholders*), even if the bordering property is part of a different borough. Decisions for the BMM will happen as part of the local committee for a given block. The public ways and any utilities on the public ways will be operated by one or more contracted companies decided on by the BMM stakeholds.
    * **20.C.190.B** **Voting**. The voting weight in the block management monopolex for each monopolex stakeholder is based on the total area of land bordering within 100 feet of the way. A monopolex stakeholder can participate in the decisions for the management monopolex for multiple blocks, but must declare what mutually exclusive fraction of their qualifying property they will be used to vote for one vs the other.
    * Periodically, prospective operators submit proposals including a secret bids to operate some aspect of the public way, the block stakeholders then uses score voting to vote on which proposal they want to take. The operator then has the right to operate and charge for services according to the proposal, and sell its services to adjacent sections of infrastructure (eg roads or utilities). 
    * The available prices for use by adjacent sections of utility must be the same options as given to the properties that make up the block. 
    * Borough ordinances may specify standards of interoperation to ensure utility sections operated by different businesses can compatibly interact. 
  * **20.C.200** Park and nature space management monopolex:
    * Operates exactly the same way as the block management monopolex, except voting power over the management of each designated park or nature space is given to the holders of land within 400 feet of the space. Votes are weighted by the area of land within 400 feet of the space (any fraction of a property farther than that is not included).
  * **20.C.210** Utility monopolex:
    * Utility and infrastructure networks are broken up into sections and competing sections can be barred from having substantially overlapping direct or indirect ownership. Competing sections could then be privately owned and operated. 
    * Borough ordinances may specify standards of interoperation to ensure utility sections operated by different businesses can compatibly interact. Note that because methods and means cannot be rewarded or punished, this means that only standards of compatibility can be required and any standard specification may only be enforced in the case that the relevant competitors can't come to an agreement on a different specification. 
  * **20.C.220** Insurance monopolex: 
    * Insurance that matches narrowly defined criteria can be required to be beholden to joint decisions by the consortium of insurance providers that have sold insurance of that kind. A 65% vote on a policy can bind the group of insurers to pay for implementation of the policy, proportional to their vote weight. Each insurance provider is given a voting weight depending on the proportion of relevant liability of the insurance contracts.
* **20.C.230** Only the following categories of visitation and residency may be given separate legislative requirements, processes, and privileges:
  * Guest Access: Someone authorized to enter and move freely in the borough for 1 month after being given or renewing Guest Access status. 
  * Trial Access: Someone authorized to enter and move freely in the borough for 1 year after being given Trial Access status.
  * Permanent Access: Someone authorized to enter and move freely in the borough indefinitely.
  * Citizenship: Someone with Permanent Access who is given direct privileges to affect the governance of the city.
* **20.C.240** Any person expelled from the borough must be given at least 1 month to personally vacate the borough and must be given at least 1 year to sell any property the ordinances requires them to sell. 
* **20.C.250** **Right to choose private ordinances.** Any person bound by the constitution of this borough may agree with any other person to an alternate set of rules that override the ordinances of the borough in any arbitration case that arises between those agreeing parties. 

### 30. Judicial branch

**Limitations to Powers:**

In any court case using default borough laws:

* **30.A** The filing party (the plaintiff in non-appeals cases, the party who lost the original case in an appeals case) pays all of the court fees.
* **40.B** 
  * The `L` likelihood of getting caught and convicted in section 7.H of the city constitution will be estimated for each of the following tiers of harm done:
    * 0 to 1/1000th the median yearly income
    * 1/1000th to 1/100ths
    * 1/100ths to 1/20ths
    * 1/20ths to 1/4th
    * 1/4th and up
  * From the likelihoods in these tiers, this can then be modified for the individual circumstances in the ways described in the city constitution. 
* **40.C** If evidence beyond a reasonable doubt is found that the defendant caused the estimated harm, the `primary assessed harm`  (`PAH`) will be the lower of `DH/L + OB ` or `DH + S/300 * DH^(0.5)` with variables defined as in City Constitution 7.H, and `S` defined as the median salary in the borough. The cost of any punishments to the offender should be as near as feasible to `PAH + AFF * AF`. 
  * If the filing party wins, `F` is set at `(1-(DO / DH)) / 2`  with a minimum value of 0,
    * where the `Defendant Offer` (`DO`) is the highest formal settlement offer made by the defendant prior to trial. 
  * If the filing party loses, `F` is set at `(DH - PO) / (2*DH)` with a minimum value of 0, 
    * where the `Plaintiff Offer` (`PO`) is the lowest formal settlement offer made by the plaintiff prior to trial.
* **40.D** If the highest standard of evidence found that the defendant caused the estimated harm is clear and convincing, the court must order an outcome costing an amount equivalent to between 30% and 70% of the amount required for outcomes obtaining evidence beyond a reasonable doubt.
* **40.E** If clear and convincing evidence is found that the defendant has not caused the claimed harm, the court must order the plaintiffs to pay the defendants 2% of the sought damages. 
* **40.F** If evidence beyond a reasonable doubt is found that the defendant has not caused the claimed harm must order the plaintiffs to pay the defendants 10% of the sought damages. 

### 50. Executive branch

**50.A** A borough president will be elected in the same way the city Mayor is elected. The Borough president organizes the implementation of the laws. 

## 60. Limitations on all persons

Security

* **60.A** Detainment, property search, and property seizure may only be performed by those who:
  * A. have completed and passed a relevant training course that fits standards determined by law, and
  * B. record video of the duration of performing any of those actions as well as the reasonable suspicion or probable cause that prompted their use.
