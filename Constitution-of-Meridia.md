# Constitution of Meridia

This document describes the primary governance of Meridia under the organizational structure of Meridia Stewardship LLC. This document goes along with a discussion document that provides context and justifications for many of the provisions herein. 

## Declaration of freedom

We hold these truths to be clearly evident, that collective government is a useful but dangerous structure, prone to exceed its immediate need and perpetuate a myriad of sufferable evils; that any involuntary interaction must be taken only with the greatest of consideration and justification; that all people must be allowed to be at the helm of their own life, take full advantage of their liberty, and pursue happiness in any way that doesn't come at the expense of others. In order to form a more perfect union, we the founders of Meridia do establish this constitution for united boroughs of Meridia to provide a limited and constrained government for the common defense, wellbeing, and prosperity of the people who will live therein, and hopefully many more that this establishment will inspire. 

## Definitions

**10.A** The definitions in the Meridia Stewardship LLC Operating Agreement in section 1.6 apply to this constitution. 

**10.B** The "Freestead" will refer to Meridia Stewardship LLC. 

**10.C** *Borough Jurisdictional Area* - The land governed by a particular borough. 

**10.D** *Borough Citizen Resident* - A Meridia Resident who has slept over night inside the Borough Jurisdictional Area of the borough in question during at least 180 days of the last 365 days.

**10.E** *Ordinance* - In the context of this constitution, "ordinance" means any provisions created contractually for Signatories via this constitution, the stewardship's operating agreement, or any documents that derive from those. 

**10.F** *Arbitral permit* - This is a permit granting special permission to someone based on an arbitration decision.

## Freestead Structure and Powers

**20.** The Freestead may only use powers that are explicitly enumerated in this constitution. Powers may not be delegated from one branch to another.

### Legislative branch

**30.** There are two legislative bodies: the council and local committees.

**40. Council:**

* **40.A** Council members are elected in a way of that borough's choosing. The vote-weight each council member receives in the council is also determined by the borough. Each borough may have no more than 3 council members. 
* **40.B** Council votes are counted using three different borough weighting methods:
  * **Resident weighting**: The total vote weight of a borough's council members will be proportional to the number of Borough Citizen Residents for the borough. 
  * **Value weighting**: The total vote weight of a borough's council members will be proportional to the total land value within the borough.
  * **Land area weighting**: The total vote weight of a borough's council members will be proportional to the total land area within the borough.
* **40.C** The council can only take official actions 2 months out of the year.
* **40.D** The council may not vote to initiate any proposal while any proposal passed by the local committee longer than 3 months ago can be voted on by the council. 

**50. Local committees:**

* **50.A** Local committees are made up of resident citizens and land holders. Each borough defines how local committees operate in their jurisdiction. They must allow at least 16 hours per year where official committee actions may be taken.
* **50.B** Like council members, votes are weighted in three different ways:
  * Weighting methods:
    * **Resident weighting**: Every Borough Citizen Resident gets equal weight. 
    * **Land value weighting**: Every voter has a vote weight equal to the **value** of land they hold in the borough. 
    * **Land area weighting**: Every voter has a vote weight equal to the **area** of land they hold in the borough. 
* **50.C** When a vote for action passes in one local committee, all local committees in directly bordering jurisdictions are expected to discuss and vote on the action within the next 4 months. A committee that is passed due on this expectation may not propose any new legislative actions until the expectation is fulfilled. This requirement is dropped when the action is decided.
* **50.D** When enough local committees have voted on an action to determine the outcome, the proposal passes for the local committees. 

**60. Council and local committee actions:**

* **60.A** **Vote counting** 
  * **60.A.10** An "X% **passing vote**" in this section means that the legislative body in question passes the proposal with a vote of X% using all three weighting methods. 
  * **60.A.20** An "X% **limiting vote**" in this section means that **either legislative body** passes a proposed *Freestead limitation* or *borough limitation* with a vote of X% using **any one** of the three weighting methods. 
  * **60.A.30** An "X% **constitutional vote**" in this section means that local committees pass the proposal with a vote of X% using all three weighting methods and X% of the boroughs pass the proposal as well.
* **60.B** **Types of legislative actions**. 
  * **60.B.10** **Normal ordinance**. A normal ordinance can be created with a 60% passing vote in both legislative bodies and can be repealed with a 50% passing vote in both legislative bodies. 
  * **60.B.20** **Limiting ordinance**. A ordinance that only contains government limitations can be created with a 50% limiting vote. A limiting ordinance can be repealed with a 60% passing vote in both legislative bodies. 
  * **60.B.30** **Freestead limitation**. A *Freestead limitation* can be created with at least a 50% passing vote in both legislative bodies. A Freestead limitation may only limit the Freestead's and/or borough's powers and may not place further limits on the people. A Freestead limitation can only be repealed, partially or fully, with a passing vote in both legislative bodies greater than the minimum of the 6 vote counts achieved (population vs value vs area weighted and council vs local committees), up to a maximum of 75%. 
  * **60.B.40** **Constitutional amendment**. A constitutional amendment can be created if a 75% constitutional vote passes.
  * **60.B.50** **Constitutional violation sanction reduction**. The local committees can reduce or forgive the sanctions for the finding of liability for a constitutional violation with a 75% passing vote.
  * **60.B.60** **Judicial consortium procedure**. The local committees can, with a 60% passing vote, create procedures for choosing the judges for judicial consortiums, and security companies to enforce rulings by a judicial consortiums. 
* **60.C** **Legislative limits**. 
  * **60.C.10** Up to 2000 words of ordinance can be proposed per year by each legislative body.
  * **60.C.20** Up to 4000 words of ordinance can be proposed for repeal per year. This includes bills to amend a ordinance which only includes striking line items.
  * **60.C.30** Up to 2000 words of Freestead limitation can be proposed per year by each legislative body.
  * **60.C.40** Up to 400 words of constitutional change can be proposed per year by each legislative body.
  * **60.C.50** A preponderance of evidence must show that an ordinance is expected to produce more total benefits than total costs (including non-monetary ones) before it can be enacted into the ordinances.
  
* **60.D** Any proposed change to the ordinances or constitution must be first proposed and made public, and no vote on the proposition may take place earlier than 3 months after it is publicly proposed.
* **60.E** A proposal passed in either legislative body expires after 3 years. 
* **60.F** A proposal that is 80% similar to a proposal that received enough votes to determine that it can not pass, even with more votes cast, not be voted on by the other body for at least 1 year afterward. 
* **60.G** While any pending arbitration order of legal clarification is waiting for council action, the council may not vote on any proposal that isn't related to a pending legal clarification order. 
* **60.H** Until 5 years after the Freestead achieved more than 20 permanent residents, the council can take official actions 6 months out of the year and can propose up to 6,000 words of ordinance, and 6,000 words of Freestead and borough limitations per year. 

**70. Legislative powers:**

* **70.A** Creating ordinance defining equivalences of harm for the violation of Freestead ordinances within the Freestead Area. 
* **70.B** Land use designation:
  * **70.B.1** Designating a minimal network of travel ways.
  * **70.B.2** Creating minimum standards for Freestead-designated travel ways, for the purposes of safety and jurisdictional clarity. 
  * **70.B.3** Creating visual and informational standards to clearly differentiate travel ways that meet Freestead standards from those that don't (eg borough-designated travel ways where the borough decides not to meet Freestead standards). 
  * **70.B.4** Designating certain land areas as protected nature-space or park-space and creating minimum standards for the use of such protected space.
  * **70.B.5** Creating boroughs from land areas greater than 25 acres that are not currently within any existing borough.
  * **70.B.6** **Land acquisition**. Admitting new land into the Freestead Area. Purchasing land within 1 mile of its border. Selling or leasing land it owns to Freestead citizens. Sale of land must include a deed restriction that subordinates the land to the terms of the Stewardship's operating agreement. Leases longer than 10 years must retain the right to collect full ground rent from the property.
  * **70.B.7** Creating processes for land valuation.
* **70.C** Dispute resolution and security
  * **70.C.10** Creating requirements and standards the Freestead's chosen arbitration services and security services must meet.
  * **70.C.20** Contracting with security companies that in total may not be paid more in a given 6 month period than the average 6 month budget of the borough with the largest security budget in the last 3 years. These security personnel may only be used to enforce rulings by the Freestead arbitration. 
* **70.D** **Freestead Admission**. Creating requirements and procedures for visitation in, residency in, land holdership in, citizenship of, and expulsion from the Freestead. 
* **70.E** **Levies**. Collecting a levy from boroughs. The levy collected must be specified as a single percentage that specifies the fraction of the total levies collected by each given borough that will be allocated to the Freestead for its use. 
* **70.F** **Weapons**. Placing limitations, for the purpose of safety and security, on the possession of physical items that would allow an average untrained person to A. kill or maim more than 30 people within a minute, or B. cause more than $100,000 of damage within a minute.

**75. Administrative powers:**

* **75.A** **Basic administration**. The legislature he ability to hire people, purchase or rent property reasonably required to administer their activities in utilizing their other granted powers. 
* **75.B** **Administrative rules**. The Freestead council and local committees have the ability set the policies for how their offices will be run. A passing vote in the Freestead council may enact such rule changes for the operation of their offices. A passing vote in the local committees may enact such rule changes for an individual local committee. 

### Judicial branch

**80. Arbitration:**

* **80.A** **Market arbitration**. The judicial branch generally consists of privately operated arbitration services selected by the legislature to judge disputes between people of different boroughs or between a person and a borough's government. 
* **80.B** **Statutory law**. Arbitration proceedings on behalf of the Freestead will be conducted using statutory law based on this constitution and ordinances and other documents produced using its processes. Common law is not used. Precedent may be used in argument, but is not legally binding. 
* **80.C** **Constitutional and ordinance interpretation**. Ordinances and contracts may be interpreted by any defendant in any way a reasonable person is likely to interpret it. A provision from this constitution or borough constitutions must be interpreted with the literal meaning intended when the provision was added to the constitution.  
* **80.D** **Jury option**. All arbitration proceedings must provide an option for a jury of no less than 6 to decide a case. Such an option must be decided by a vote of jurors and arbitrators where no more than 20% of the vote outcome may be influenced by arbitrators. At least 50% of the fee must be paid by the party who demanded the jury.

**90. Freestead arbitration powers:**

* **90.A** **Investigate orders**. Issuing an order for search, seizure, compulsion of appearing as a witness in an arbitration hearing, and compulsion of furnishing evidence in an arbitration hearing, where allowed by government law. Such orders may be used to apply for judicial assistance or enforcement under applicable law from a court of relevant jurisdiction. 

* **90.B** **Order of clarification**. Ordering the legislature to update an ordinance to clarify an ambiguity. In such a case, any existing cases in which that ambiguity arose should not be delayed on the basis of such an order and update to the ordinances should not affect the case, unless both plaintiff and defendant agree to the delay and for the update to apply to their case.

* **90.C** **Deciding jurisdiction**. Determining the jurisdiction in which a dispute will be decided when there is ambiguity in what the jurisdiction for a particular case should be. This may include splitting a case into multiple cases if different parts of the case should be handled in different jurisdictions.

* **90.D** Determining the outcome of disputes between a person and a borough's government, including appealing borough arbitration decisions.

* **90.E** **Available remedies**. Outcomes ordered by the arbitrators fall into the category of compulsory outcomes and optional outcomes. 
  The compulsory outcomes may be any of the following:
  
  * Transfer of money or property from the culprit:
    * to the damaged person, or
    * to the party that incurred costs to bring them to justice.
  * Loss of citizenship.
  * Temporary or permanent expulsion from the Freestead Area.
  * Release of the plaintiff to seek remedy utilizing government law.
  * Release of the plaintiff to seek remedy in a government court. 
  
  Optional outcomes may be offered to the defendant in lieu of a compulsory outcome. If the defendant does not follow through with the requested optional outcomes, a predetermined compulsory outcome will be ordered.
  
  * Optional rehabilitation training or therapeutic activity for the culprit.
  * Optional confinement to their residence.
  * Optional probation. 
  
* **90.F** **Clear and convincing evidence.** To order an outcome against a person that involves only a transfer of money there must be clear and convincing evidence, according to the ordinances at the time of the act, that the person was at fault and that the harm done was not lower than estimated.

* **90.G** **Evidence beyond a reasonable doubt**. To order an outcome against a person that involves something other than a transfer of money it must be beyond a reasonable doubt that, according to the ordinances at the time of the act, the person was at fault and that the harm done was not lower than estimated.

* **90.H** **Cost to the defendant**. The cost of any arbitration outcomes to the defendant may not exceed `(DH + LH*L + SC)/LG + OB + AF * AFF`, where
  
  * the "cost of any arbitration outcomes", includes any arbitrator-ordered outcomes, any time the person spent detained in a citizen's arrest or by government police, and time spent in a government jail or prison, and time in arbitration proceedings. 
  * `DH` is the Direct Harm done to the victim. This does not include any costs to the victim in bringing suit to the offender. 
  * `LH` is the average Likely Harm that might have been done to the victim (but did not).
  * `L` is the Likelihood that the relevant significant harm would have happened.
  * `SC` is the Societal Cost, the estimated average or marginal cost that this type of harm places on society (those other than victims who have brought suit) for each instance.
  * `LG` is the Likelihood of getting caught and found liable.
    * Things such as incidence (ratio of incidents caught divided by estimated total incidents), intent (negligence vs premeditation), ability to or effort made to avoid getting caught or minimize evidence, level of ability to or effort made to avoid harm in the case of negligence, and the like can be used to justify the level of this likelihood.
    * `LG` must be no lower than 1% - if it would be lower than 1%, the value 1% should be used.
  * `OB` is the Offender Benefit, any significant measurable benefits the offender receives from the decided sanctions (eg rehabilitation care, training, education, etc).
  * `AF` are the Arbitration Fees.
  * `AFF` is the Arbitration Fee Fraction - the fraction of arbitration fees the defendant can be ordered to pay. This fraction may not exceed 50% and `AF * AFF` may not exceed 5 times `DH`.
  
* **90.I** **Cost to the Freestead**. The cost to the Freestead of any sanctions of the offender may not exceed `DE * (DH + SC - OG) + OB` where
  
  * `DH`, `SC`, and `OB` are as above.
  * `DE` (deterrence effectiveness) is the percent reduction of harm of this type being done as a result of imposing the sanctions in question.
  * `OG` is the offender-gain, an estimated quantification of the value the offender would have gained from their harm-causing actions if not caught.
  
* **90.J** Any ordered transfer of money to a party is given as collection right to the recipient or recipients, who can then organize or sell that right as they see fit. 

* **90.K** Any outcome that is not money-transfer is organized by the Freestead executive branch. 

* **90.L** Arbitrators have the discretion to dismiss a case where the harm estimated is below the arbitration fees.

* **90.M** **Manifest harm and risk imposition**. Harm done to a victim must be based on either actual harm done or risk imposition. In the case of risk imposition, this must be a concrete physical state or condition created by the person who allegedly caused harm by risk imposition that increases the probability of manifest harm, and may not be based on that person's estimated mental state but only on their demonstrated competency and ability. For a freestead arbitration proceeding to find harm done from risk imposition, there must be evidence beyond a reasonable doubt that the expected risk imposition is not lower than estimated.

**100. Judicial consortium:** 

**100.A** When there is a dispute involving officials or actions of the Freestead government or a Freestead arbitration, the case must first be decided in borough arbitration. Regardless of the decision, the defendant or the plaintiff may appeal, and upon appeal a judicial consortium is formed. A judge is chosen by each borough to make up the judicial consortium (via the processes set up by local committees). The consortium then presides and decides the dispute by either validating the decision made, dismissing it, or amending it. 

**110. Review board:**

**110.A** When the Freestead's population is greater than 120, once per year, a review board of Freestead citizens is selected consisting of 1 judge chosen by each borough in a manner of its choosing and the lesser of 30 citizens or 10% of the Freestead's citizens selected randomly in equal proportions from each borough. 

**110.B** The board will review the conduct of Freestead officials, arbitrators, and security personnel. Members of the board can nominate individuals of these types for a hearing, during which the person in question will hear the review, the board will ask that official questions, and the person is given opportunity to make statements clarifying their conduct. Once the hearing is complete, the board will vote on whether to sanction the person and to what degree. 

**110.C** The board's vote on whether to sanction an official and whether to accept a proposed sanction is weighted such that the vote of the judges have 10% of the voting power and the rest of the board has 90% of the voting power. A sanction is approved with a 75% vote of the review board affirming that there is clear and convincing evidence of liability.

**110.D** Sanctions may include removal from office, a fine up to twice the yearly pay of the given official, loss of citizenship, and temporary expulsion from the Freestead Area of up to 3 years.

**110.E** Any selectee may decline further participation at any point, after which they can no longer rejoin. When this happens, either voluntarily or for reasons of incapacitation or absence, a replacement is chosen by the same process as used for original selection. 

### 120. Executive branch

**120.A** An executive branch made up of a Mayor who organizes the implementation of ordinances. The mayor is elected in a score vote by the local committees, where the total score for a candidate is found by multiplying together the average scores for all three weightings (value, area, and population) for that candidate. 

**120.B** The Mayor may also organize investigations, arbitration proceedings, or lawsuits in a government court (when authorized by an arbitration proceeding) against those who cause damages to the Freestead government's property. 

**120.C Administrative powers:**

* **120.C.10** **Basic administration**. The executive branch has the ability to hire people, purchase or rent property reasonably required to administer their activities in utilizing their other granted powers. 
* **120.C.20** **Administrative rules**. The mayor has the ability set the policies for how the executive offices will be run.

## 130. Borough powers

**130.A** A borough may only use powers that are explicitly enumerated in this constitution. Boroughs may use the following powers:

* **130.B** **Property rights**. Creating ordinances defining property rights, contract law, equivalences of harm (ie assigning an equivalent monetary value to a particular amount of a type of harm).
* **130.C** **Guardianship**. Creating ordinances defining guardianship rights and responsibilities. 
* **130.D** **Promoting competition**. Creating ordinances that promote the existence of multiple independent competitors in a market.
* **130.E** **Freestead council**. Creating constraints on council member eligibility, term length, and selection process.
* **Levies and externalities**:
  * **130.F10** **Correcting externalities**. Creating ordinances for the purpose of correcting positive and negative real (nonpecuniary) externalities.
  * **130.F20** **Ground rent**. Collection of a ground rent proportional to land value or real externalities of land .
  * **130.G** **Tax reimbursement**. Reimbursing taxes from levels of government above the Freestead level in the form of direct payments or in the form of levy reductions. 
* **Arbitration**
  * **130.H** **Arbitration standards**. Creating requirements and standards for the borough arbitration system.
  * **130.I** **Investigative orders**. Issuing an order for search, seizure, compulsion of appearing as a witness in an arbitration hearing, and compulsion of furnishing evidence in an arbitration hearing, where allowed by government law. Such orders may be used to apply for judicial assistance or enforcement under applicable law from a court of relevant jurisdiction.
  * **130.J** **Court permission**. Granting a plaintiff the right to pursue a case in a arbitration at a higher-level than the Freestead, only when remedies ordered within Freestead or borough ordinances have become infeasible to enforce within the Freestead Area.
* **Security**
  * **130.K** **Security standards**. Creating requirements and standards for security companies the borough government uses.
  * **130.L** **Surveillance on Freestead property**. Creating a procedure and requirements to allow people or entities to place surveillance equipment on public ways or other public property. Such equipment must be placed for a particular purpose or purposes, and only used for those granted purposes. The purpose may only be for gathering evidence of a real externality (eg noise cameras or surveillance for the purposes of obtaining evidence of property damage). 
* **Emergencies**
  * **130.M** **Emergency procedures**. Creating procedures to choose private groups to hire for the alleviation of emergencies. 
  * **130.N** **Emergency borrowing**. Borrowing money for the purposes of alleviating an emergency that affects more than 100 people. The Borough may not borrow more than 20% of its yearly levy revenue, and any borrowed money must be paid back within 4 years.
* **130.O** **Economic assistance programs**. Creating welfare programs with total of yearly payouts that may not exceed 10% of the borough's yearly levy revenue and also may not exceed 0.1% of the total GDP of the borough.
* **Land use**
  * **130.P** **Externality-based zoning**. Creating zones that have differentiated ordinances related to real externalities.
  * **130.Q** Designating additional travel ways beyond Freestead-designated ways.
  * **130.R** Creating additional minimum standards for Freestead and borough travel ways and utilities within the borough. 
  * **130.S** Designating certain land as protected nature-space or park space.
  * **130.T** **Borough Admission**. Creating requirements and procedures for visitation in, residency in, land holdership in, citizenship of, and expulsion from the borough. 
* **Licensure and information requirements**
  * **130.U** **Externality preventative licensure**. Creating licensure requirements for doing certain activity likely to cause negative real externalities, including but not limited to dog ownership, operation of heavy equipment that weighs more than 300 pounds or can perform more than 10 kilowatts of work, handling hazardous materials, demolition work, construction, and security. 
  * **130.V** **Addictive activity and self-reinforcing harm**. Creating licensure requirements for doing certain activity likely to trigger natural mechanisms in the brain and body in unnatural ways known to lead to significantly harmful effects to the person doing the activity in more than 1/1000th of instances, and creating labeling or other information disclosures requirements for those providing those activities as a product or service, including but not limited to gambling, drugs, hormones and endocrine disruptors, porn, use of social media, and processed or engineered foods. 
  * **130.W** Licensure requirements can be in the form of training requirements, competency demonstration, and ongoing performance of the licensed activities. 
  * **130.X** **Speedy licensing**. Whenever any training or competency demonstration are required for a license, the government must ensure there is an option to fulfil this that costs no more than 2 times the median daily income of a Freestead resident and is expected to take no more than 30 hours to complete. If such an option does not exist at any time, people are free to perform the relevant actions without a license for the period of time between when the required option ceased to be available until 30 days after a required option becomes available again. 
  * **130.Y** A borough may prohibit people from assisting a ward in performing any activity that requires a license without approval of their guardian, or selling a ward a service that requires certain information disclosure without approval of their guardian.

## 140. Freestead and borough restrictions

* **140.A** Violations and attempted violations of the Freestead constitution and borough constitutions will be treated as serious harm and will have serious consequences. A constitutional offense is defined as any decision or supporting vote by an official of or acting under the direct or indirect direction of a Freestead or borough government for an ordinance, decision, or action that is later determined to violate the Freestead constitution or a borough constitution by a judicial consortium. A person found liable of a constitutional violation is suspended from Freestead or borough office or position and the action is considered damage equivalent to between 1-20% of their wealth and may result in temporary or permanent expulsion from the Freestead Area. 
* **140.B** Property rights or other laws defined at higher levels of government than the Freestead cannot be enforced or applied by Freestead, borough, or private arbitrators selected as part of this constitution without a constitutionally valid arbitral order allowing application of such law. In general, enforcement of those laws is left to county, state, and federal courts and police and all claims arising under Texas, Federal, or County law are expressly excluded and split from claims adjudicated by Freestead or Borough arbitrators unless the aforementioned arbitral order is obtained. 
* **140.C** Ordinances passed in a borough around negative real externalities (including intrusion of matter and energy such as sound, light, smells, etc) will be respected by those at the border in a different borough, and that borough will also have rights to benefit from active ordinances in an adjacent borough around positive real externalities. 
* **140.D** A borough may be between 30 and 600 acres (inclusive) and no part of a borough be more than 3 miles from any other part of the borough.
* **140.E** **Consequentialist law**. No ordinance that requires, incentivizes, disincentivizes, or makes exceptions for any particular method, process, procedure, technology, design, intention, or action for private people (ie signatories other than in their capacity as an agent of the Freestead or a borough) can be enacted. All ordinances that make requirements on private citizens must define the requirement in terms of the material results, outcomes, qualities, and metrics in terms of how something affect others.
  * The following are narrowly defined exceptions to this restriction: for licensing requirements, welfare requirements, tax reimbursement requirements, arbitration standards, security standards, and requirements for freestead and borough admission. 

**140.G** **Freestead and borough restrictions expressed as rights and freedoms of private persons:**

A "right" in this section means that a Freestead or borough cannot put any barriers (eg requirements, fees, fines), given any incentives (eg payment or grants of privileges), or place other consequences in place for someone to engage in that right, not engage in that right, or engage in that right in a particular way, excepting the constitutional powers of requiring licensure, information disclosure, and limitations regarding wards listed elsewhere in this document. 

* **140.G.10** **Right to consensually interact**, contract, and act in any way that harms no other person. 
* **140.G.20** **Right to contract with covenants**. A covenant is only valid as long as there is at all times some entity with the contractual power to alter or remove the covenant. 
* **140.G.30** **Freedom of thought**, expression, religion, and assembly in ways that do not directly cause immediate harm to another.
* **140.G.40** **Right to sue**, including the right to sue officials and agents of the Freestead and boroughs for breach of constitution, ordinance, or private contract that person is party to. The Freestead and boroughs do not have sovereign immunity and cannot grant immunity from suit to anyone. 
* **140.G.50** **Freedom to be assumed innocent until proven guilty**. One must never have to legally defend oneself until the party bringing up charges or suit has been determined to have a case that would not end in favor of the accused without legal defense.
* **140.G.60** **Borough creation**. Right to create a new borough or join an adjacent borough as a group. This requires a contiguous block of land containing at least 50 adult residents and at least 25 acres and requires approval by a land-value weighted 75% vote by the land holders as well as a land-area weighted 75% vote by the land holders. Any successful vote on this subject will take effect in a timeframe to be specified by Freestead ordinance, no sooner than 4 months after the successful vote. When it takes effect, the land involved in the group will secede from any borough they are part of.
* **140.G.70** **Right to exit**. No person may be prevented from leaving a borough or the Freestead except as is required for being lawfully detained for suspected harm when legally permitted.
* **140.G.80** **Loss of status**. 
  * In the case an arbitration hearing orders temporary expulsion, the person being expelled must be given at least 1 month's time to prepare for leaving the Freestead Area. During the time they are expelled, they will be treated as trespassing if they enter the Freestead Area. 
  * In the case an arbitration hearing orders removal of citizenship, the person who's citizen status is being revoked has 1 year to transfer any land or real property they hold in the Freestead Area to a citizen. After 1 year, if the property in question is still not held by a Freestead citizen, the property may not be occupied until it is held by such.
  * In the case an arbitration hearing orders permanent expulsion, the person being expelled has any citizenship status they have removed and will be given at least 1 month's time to prepare for leaving the Freestead Area unless they present a serious risk of causing significant additional harm to people in the Freestead Area. 
* **Dispute rights**
  * **140.G.90** Right to due process. Including freedom from double jeopardy, attainder, ex post facto litigation, being compelled to be a witness against oneself, and excessive bail. Also including the right to a public and speedy arbitration procedure with impartial arbitrators.
  * **140.G.100** Right to equal protections under this constitution, irrespective of social status, governmental position, or any other circumstance irrelevant to the evaluation of the ordinance in question. 
  * **140.G.110** Right to an arbitration decision by an impartial jury of those local to where the harm was alleged in any case where non-monetary transfer outcomes are possible or where the harm done is claimed to have a monetary equivalent of more than 10% of the wealth of the accused or more than $10,000 (2024$).
  * **140.G.120** Right to choice in dispute resolution. When defaulting to Freestead or borough decided arbitration company, at least 3 choices of arbitration companies must be offered to the accused. The arbitration companies offered must have no overlap in direct or indirect ownership.

### 150. Limitations on all persons

These are limitations on all signatories within the Freestead Area, regardless of legal status or any other status.

* Use of force
  * **150.A** Use of force may only be used when the exercise of persuasion, advice, and warning is found to be insufficient to protect the rights of oneself or another. When force is used, a force may be used that is no greater than the minimum degree of physical force a reasonable person would think is necessary to protect the rights of oneself or another.
* Security
  * Enforcement
    * **150.B** Enforcement by private security may proceed through any mechanism authorized by law, including lawful private enforcement and judicial assistance.
  * Detainment
    * **150.C** No person or property may be detained without reasonable suspicion of the unremediated violation of someone else's rights and being informed of such and the related probable cause.
    * **150.D** No person or property may be detained for longer than reasonable suspicion exists nor longer than it should take to determine whether probable cause exists. 
  * Search
    * **150.E** Forcible search of items on a person may not be done without the person being lawfully detained.
    * **150.F** No property may be forcibly searched and no court order for that purpose may be sought, without obtaining and producing an arbitral permit establishing and recording probable cause, the place to be searched, what is expected to be found, and reasonable suspicion that items will be found that are not legally possessed or are evidence of a violation. 
  * Seizure 
    * **150.G** No property, other than weapons of someone detained and only during the detention, may be seized, and no court order for that purpose may be sought, without obtaining and producing an arbitral permit establishing and recording probable cause and the items to be seized. Property may not be seized without probable cause that either the property is not legally possessed or that seizure is necessary as evidence for the relevant case.
  * Arrest
    * **150.H** Seeking the police to arrest someone is only permitted if the reasonably estimated flight risk is more than 30% when evaluated against the reasonably estimated damage done, or if risk of that person committing harm is reasonably estimated to be higher than 20%. The person to be arrested must be informed of these details. 
  * Compensation
    * **150.I** A person detained, forcibly searched, or subject to seizure, regardless of the outcome of any related court or arbitration case, is owed compensation proportional to the time and level of inconvenience by the party that inconvenienced them. In the case that police are utilized, inconvenience caused by the police will be considered to be the responsibility of the person who utilized the police for that purposes, or the entity that person is the agent of. This compensation may include a reduction in remedies that burden the person in question.
