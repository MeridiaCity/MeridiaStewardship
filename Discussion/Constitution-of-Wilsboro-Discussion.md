# Constitution of Wilsboro Discussion

## Summary

* No substantial bureaucracy. The executive branch primarily functions as an administrator that manages  and directs finances.
* Tragedy of the commons and the free rider problem is solved in two ways:
  * via the borough government subsidizing insurance that cause positive externalities, and
  * via rewarding private individuals who successfully identify and prove harm.
* Natural monopolies and similar situations where healthy competition doesn't naturally happen is solved in one primary way eg for utilities:
  * sections of utilities are collectively owned by the residents adjacent to each section of utility, and those residents vote to decide what company will operate that section of utility. 

## Powers and limitations

### 20. Legislative branch

**20.C Limitations to Powers:**

* **20.C.20** 
  * **Building height limits**. While its likely that this can be expressed in a way that matches the above description about energy and matter, eg light being blocked from adjacent properties, allowing some regulation of building heights seems to be a very practical mechanism to use. Unless a very clearly superior way to specify this without building heights comes along, building heights seem reasonable to regulate.
* **20.C.80** **Economic assistance**. 

  * This limits welfare to people who have been in (and presumably contributed to) the community for some time already, and limits the risk of some people coming in to take advantage of the welfare and then taking those earnings out of the community.
  * When the tax money for welfare comes from a land value tax, the tapering of a negative income tax has [effects similar to a tax](https://micahe.substack.com/p/means-testing-is-an-inefficient-tax?r=1fcmli) which causes deadweight losses. UBI does not have this problem when funded with LVT (tho it would if it were funded with other taxes).
* **20.C.90** Some use of excess funds should be specified, but it is not recommended that land value taxes be reduced substantially from their ideal 100%, because doing so increases deadweight losses. Finding ways to reimburse the producers of positive externalities would be a better use of funds. A citizens' dividend.
* **20.C.190** Block management monopolex (BMM)
  * Requiring the prices given to adjacent blocks be the same as those given to the properties on the block minimizes the possibility of the owners of one section setting monopolistic pricing on an adjacent section - if they have to pay that price too, they'll be more fair about the price.
* **20.C.220** Insurance monopolex
  * An example of this would be a trash cleanliness insurance monopolex for a particular area. The borough (or perhaps a block management monopolex) could reverse-auction a particular insurance contract, for example that requires insurance payout proportional to a trash count conducted periodically. Multiple insurance companies could participate in that auction and bid down the cost of contracts. The winners of the auction would then be paid for the contracts and assume the liability of paying out on trash counts. Those insurance companies would then have an incentive to reduce trash in that area in various ways, both preventative and retroactive. 

### 30. Judicial branch

**Limitations to Powers:**

* **40.C** 
  * While the city constitution allows including the "societal cost" in the harm, defendants shouldn't have the cost of any deterrence or included in their fine because as long as the fine is set as listed above, then net cost to society should always be 0, rendering any personal deterrence or surveillance an individual employs strictly a choice that benefits that individual in the form of risk reduction or the like.
  * The power of `0.5` above is to allow a higher ratio of punitive component for lower value fines but also to limit how much extra someone can be punished over the damage they caused for higher value harm.
  * Having the plaintiff generally pay most of the court fees incentivizes them to only take a case to court if the cost of adjudication is worth the correction. Having the non-filing party pay, in the case they lose, incentivizes them not to delay the case in an attempt to escape justice. Having the fraction of the court fees the defendant pays, in the case they lose, be related to their highest settlement offer incentivizes the defendant to make fair offers in order to avoid partially reimbursing the plaintiff for the court fees while at the same time not pushing them to accept higher-than-appropriate settlement offers from the plaintiff. `F` is set up so that at maximum, the defendant pays 50% of the court fees.

* **40.D** With a lower burden of proof standard, there is a higher likelihood the charged party is innocent, and so the economically efficient damages to charge is necessarily lower than one with a higher burden of proof.
* **40.F** Requiring the plaintiffs to pay a fraction of sought damages if its strongly found that the defendant did not caused the claimed harm compensates for the risk of a mistaken verdict conferred on defendants beyond the nominal cost of their defense (ie the likelihood that the defendant is falsely found liable). [See here for more info](http://www.daviddfriedman.com/Academic/FeudThenandNow.html).
