# **Stewardship Agreement Justifications**

## **2) Stewardship equity rights**

2.1 Allowing the Stewardship to purchase member unit gives it a way to eventually gain autonomy over Meridia. Land holders might eventually hold maybe >70% of Meridia's land. With a 1% share of Meridia LLC, and assuming revenue from ground rent is 1/30th of its current market sale value, one could expect that over 150 years, Meridia Stewardship could buy back all investment shares and become completely autonomous.

2.2 In the case the Stewardship purchases *non-voting improvement land units*, they will be converted into *voting improvement land units*. 

## **3) Stewardship rights of second refusal**

3.1 In the case that the Company decides to liquidate more than 10% of its assets or more than 10% of the land held by Meridia Land LLC, and any group of Company members does not use their right of first refusal, the Stewardship will have a right of second refusal with a time limit of 70 days from the decision to liquidate, or 40 days from an offer on those assets being accepted, whichever is longer. If the proposed consideration includes non-cash consideration that cannot reasonably be matched, the Stewardship may instead provide cash having an equivalent fair market value.

## **4) Meridia LLC voting rights**

4.1 Meridia LLC will initially be allocated 50% of each class of member unit on each reallocation, and this fraction will be reduced by 5% for each year after the first citizen unit is allocated. As clarification, this means that after 2 years, Meridia LLC will be allocated 40% of each class of member units, after 5 years 25%, and after 10 years, zero.

4.2 In any vote weighted by member units, once the vote is closed to all other Members, Meridia LLC will have 1 week to submit its votes.

## **5) Ground rent rights**

5.1 The Stewardship and the Company decide separately on their fractions to collect so that the amount to collect is cleanly calculable and one company's decision does not affect how much the other collects.

5.2 There is a fallback method to determine land's rental value for the Company so that if the Stewardship chooses a poor method that significantly underassesses land value, investors aren't left in the lurch.

5.3 The Company retains the ability to collet its own ground rent in the case the Stewardship fails to do this properly, again so investors aren't left in the lurch.

## **6) Access rights**

6.1 This puts the responsibility on the Stewardship to determine who can be in Meridia and who can hold land in Meridia. No criteria is specified so that the Stewardship has full responsibility over that.

6.2 Lender protection ensures that Meridia leases are mortgageable.
